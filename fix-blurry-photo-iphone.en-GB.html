How to unblur a photo on iPhone (and what can and cannot be fixed)

The kids finally looked at the camera and the shot came out soft. A screenshot of an address is too fuzzy to read. This guide shows how to tell what went wrong, which fix to use, how to check the result, and where the honest limits are. Step 1: work out why the photo is blurry

Blurry screenshots, receipts and documents

Text is a special case, because a sharpened face can look slightly different and still be fine, but a sharpened number has to be exactly right.

  • Use the Document mode for screenshots, photos of whiteboards, receipts and scanned pages; it is built to crisp up text rather than skin and hair.
  • Start from the largest copy you have. A screenshot forwarded through a chat app or social network is often shrunk and recompressed. Ask for the original, or take the screenshot again yourself.
  • Check every number against the source. Enhancement makes text easier to read, but an address, a phone number, an amount or a code should always be confirmed against the original before you rely on it.
  • Retake when you can. For a document in front of you, a new, steady photo in good light beats any repair: hold the phone parallel to the page and tap to focus on the text.

What can and cannot be fixed

An honest list, so you know what to expect before you start.

  • Usually fixable: light camera shake, slightly missed focus, noise from dark scenes, photos shrunk by messaging apps, and soft text.
  • Improves, up to a point: strong shake with clearly doubled edges, and very small faces. AI Super Resolution rebuilds detail, which means anything that was never captured is an estimate.
  • Not fixable: areas that are completely blown out to white or crushed to black, tiny thumbnails, and subjects that moved so much that their shape is smeared.

That is why the before and after comparison matters. Before you treat an enhanced photo as the real thing, press to compare and make sure faces and text still match the original.

Tips for cleaner results

  • Use the original file. The photo straight from your camera roll has more to work with than a copy sent through a chat app or email.
  • One mode at a time. Auto Clear first, then one targeted mode, gives a more natural look than stacking everything at once.
  • Enlarge last. Denoise and sharpen before 4× Upscale, so you do not enlarge the noise as well.
  • Brighten dark photos first. Low Light before Sharpen lets you sharpen detail you can actually see.
  • Scan old prints straight on. A crooked scan with a shadow across it restores worse than a quick, flat retake in daylight.

Frequently asked questions

Can you actually unblur a photo?

Often, yes, within limits. Light camera shake, slightly missed focus, noise and small images usually improve a lot. Detail that the camera never captured cannot be recovered, only estimated, so compare before and after.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. According to the App Store listing, Unblurry works 100% on-device: no cloud, no account, and your photos never leave your phone.

Will enlarging a small photo make it blurrier?

4× Upscale is designed to enlarge without the mush, and AI Super Resolution rebuilds detail on the device. Run Denoise first so the noise is not enlarged too.

Can it fix old printed photos?

Yes, scan or photograph the print, then use Portrait & Restore to enhance faces and revive the old photo. A straight, shadow-free scan gives the cleanest result.
